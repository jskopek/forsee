## 1. Best open TTS models in late 2026, and which run on-device in real time

### Takeaway
The realistic on-device shortlist for a cloned, streaming voice in an MIT app is: **Kyutai Pocket TTS** (100M, CPU-only, ~200 ms first audio, cloning, 7 languages, no phonemizer, MIT code), **NeuTTS Air** (Apache-2.0, GGUF, 3 s cloning, but phoneme input via espeak), **Chatterbox-Nano/Turbo** (MIT, `[laugh]` tags, but retired by Resemble in Aug 2026), **KittenTTS 2** (1.7B ternary, cloning + emotion tags, but separate "Stellon Labs Community License" for weights and chunk-level streaming only), and **Kokoro-82M** (Apache weights, no cloning). The most natural and expressive open-weight models (Fish S2-Pro / Drama 3, Higgs Audio v3, IndexTTS-2.5, F5-TTS, VoxCPM2, Qwen3-TTS 1.7B) are either licence-restricted or too heavy for tablets and GPU-less laptops; they are useful as an offline "teacher" or quality reference, not as the live engine.

### Cited Findings

#### 1a. Small models that are plausible live engines

**Kyutai Pocket TTS** [P]
- 100M parameters; English models down to 1.9M parameters; runs on CPU using only 2 cores; "~200ms to get the first audio chunk"; "~6x real-time on a CPU of MacBook Air M4"; audio streaming; voice cloning from a wav file; languages: English, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Spanish, Dutch; handles arbitrarily long text — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- New small English models (Oct 2026): `english_18m_2026-10` (18.2M), `english_7m_2026-10` (6.9M), `english_2m_2026-10` (1.9M) "decoder included", with `_refl` reward-fine-tuned variants; a "drifting" sampler variant `english_drifting_26-09` (Sep 2026). Non-English languages also have larger 24-layer variants that are "higher quality but slower" — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Standalone `pocket-tts` binary (Rust port on `xn-ptts`) for Linux, macOS (x86/ARM) and Windows x86, needing neither Python nor PyTorch; x86 needs AVX2 (an SSE4.2 `-v2` build exists); CPU only — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- GPU gave no speed-up on Apple silicon; on a 4-vCPU cloud x86 VM the CPU ran only ~2.3–2.5x real time (T4 GPU ~6.3x), i.e. weak laptop CPUs have much less headroom than an M4 — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Cloned voices can be exported once to a `.safetensors` voice state ("just reading the kvcache from disk"), so audio-prompt processing is not repeated at speak time — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Limitation: reference quality is copied ("the audio quality of the sample is also reproduced"); Kyutai recommends cleaning the sample first. Inserting pauses via text is listed as unsupported — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- No phoneme stage: FluidAudio's catalogue describes it as "No phoneme stage, works directly on text tokens" (so no espeak-ng dependency) — [FluidAudio Models.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Models.md)
- Licence: code LICENSE file is MIT-style permission text [P] — [pocket-tts LICENSE](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/LICENSE). Weights licence not verified from the model card; a secondary site lists it inconsistently as CC BY 4.0 or MIT [S] — [tts.ai Pocket TTS page](https://tts.ai/voices/pocket-tts/?lang=sv). Bundled preset voices have per-voice licences (VCTK, Expresso, EARS, voice donations) — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- README "Prohibited use" clause forbids "voice impersonation or cloning without explicit and lawful consent" — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Community-trained languages exist (Czech, Hindi, Korean, Farsi, Indonesian, Estonian, Welsh, Turkish, Polish, Greek, Japanese, Chinese, Indic multilingual) — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Related: **Gradium Phonon** 31M/40M English streaming TTS built on the same architecture; FluidAudio lists Phonon 31m as Apache-2.0 with 103 voices — [mlx-audio README](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio/blob/main/README.md), [FluidAudio Models.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Models.md)

**NeuTTS (Neuphonic): Air, Nano, 2E** [P]
- NeuTTS-Air ~360M active params (552M with embeddings), **Apache 2.0**, phoneme input. NeuTTS-Nano ~120M active (229M), English/French/German/Spanish separate models, **NeuTTS Open License 1.0**. NeuTTS-2E ~125M active, English, text input, emotional control, NeuTTS Open License 1.0 — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- NeuTTS Open License v1.0 (updated 11 Dec 2025): commercial use is conditioned on the legal entity not exceeding a US$5M annual revenue threshold; threshold does not apply to qualified non-profits for non-commercial/research purposes — [neutts LICENSE](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/LICENSE)
- "Instant voice cloning ... with as little as 3 seconds of audio"; recommended reference: mono, 16–44 kHz, 3–15 s, clean, natural continuous speech; reference transcript required — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- Context window 2048 tokens ≈ 30 s of audio including the prompt; NeuCodec is a 50 Hz single-codebook codec; output 24 kHz; GGUF Q4/Q8 quantisations; streaming only with GGUF backbones; ONNX (incl. int8) codec decoder available — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- Throughput of the language model only (Q4_0, codec excluded): Galaxy A25 5G CPU: Air 20 tok/s, Nano 45 tok/s; AMD Ryzen 9 HX 370 CPU: 119 / 221 tok/s; iMac M4 CPU: 111 / 195 tok/s — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- NeuTTS-2E: 4 fixed speakers (no cloning), emotions angry, disgusted, fearful, happy, neutral, sad, surprised — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- Latency guidance: use GGUF backbone, pre-encode the reference, use the ONNX codec decoder — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- Outputs are Perth-watermarked by default — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- Phonemizer: training guide points to the `phonemizer` espeak backend, i.e. Air/Nano depend on espeak(-ng) for G2P — [neutts TRAINING.md](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/TRAINING.md)
- Fine-tuning script provided (Llasa-style); "for a dataset of 10 hours, 1000 to 2000 steps is often sufficient", LR 1e-5 to 4e-5; custom tags such as `<|LAUGHING|>`, `<|WHISPERING|>` can be added if the data is labelled — [neutts TRAINING.md](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/TRAINING.md)

**Chatterbox family (Resemble AI)** [P]
- Banner: "As of August 2026, Chatterbox is retired research and is no longer maintained ... The code stays public as an archive under the MIT license, with no support, updates, or paid tier." Resemble now builds deepfake detection — [chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/README.md)
- Chatterbox-Turbo: 350M, English, one-step distilled decoder, native paralinguistic tags `[cough]`, `[laugh]`, `[chuckle]`. Chatterbox-Nano: 110M, same architecture and tags, "3x faster than realtime on 8 CPU cores". Chatterbox Multilingual V3: 500M, 23 languages. Original Chatterbox 500M has `exaggeration` and `cfg_weight` controls — [chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/README.md)
- Every output carries a Perth neural watermark — [chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/README.md)
- Code licence MIT — [chatterbox LICENSE](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/LICENSE). Weights licence not verified (secondary sources describe Chatterbox as MIT [S] — [findskill.ai](https://findskill.ai/blog/best-open-source-tts-2026/)).
- Core ML ports exist: FluidAudio ships "Chatterbox Multilingual (520M, 18 languages) and Chatterbox Nano (110M ...)" — [FluidAudio README](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/README.md)

**KittenTTS 2 (KittenML / Stellon Labs)** [P]
- Flagship Kitten-TTS 2: 1.7B-parameter "1-bit" (ternary) speech language model with in-context voice cloning from 5–30 s (Whisper auto-transcribes the reference), 115 built-in voices, 20 languages, 24 kHz; download 954 MB lossless or 492 MB (`emb4`) — [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md)
- Expression (beta): leading `[emotion]` tag, inline `<event>` tags (`<gasp> <giggle> <growl> <gulp> <laugh> <pause> <scoff> <sigh> <sob> <um>`), `(((emphasis)))` spans; "steers delivery rather than guaranteeing it" — [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md), [voices-and-expression.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/voices-and-expression.md)
- Requirements: "A CUDA GPU with roughly 8 GB free, or a CPU with about 6 GB of RAM"; llama.cpp fork (`kitten-tts-2-cpp`, beta) with a 1.03 GB GGUF; MLX backend for Apple silicon (default PyTorch on a Mac CPU is "below realtime") — [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md), [mlx.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/mlx.md), [kitten-tts-2-cpp README](https://github.com/KittenML/kitten-tts-2-cpp/blob/main/README.md)
- Streaming is "chunk-level, not token-level"; input under roughly 380 characters yields exactly one chunk, so a short utterance does not stream and first audio costs the full generation time — [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md)
- Small models: kitten-tts-2-mini 82M (16 languages), nano 17M (17 MB per language, 8 languages), pico 7.2M (8 MB), int8 ONNX; plus older 0.8 models (15M–80M). No cloning in these. English G2P is misaki lexicon + CMUdict + a 0.75M BART G2P; **other languages use espeak-ng** — [kitten-tts-2-tiny.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/kitten-tts-2-tiny.md); the 0.8 ONNX models need `espeakng_loader` and `phonemizer` — [onnx-models.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/onnx-models.md)
- Licence: code Apache 2.0; "The models are licensed separately"; KittenTTS 2 weights are under the "Stellon Labs Community License" (terms not readable from here) — [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md)

**Kokoro-82M** [P]
- 82M params, Apache-licensed weights, 24 kHz, preset voices only; uses misaki G2P; espeak-ng is "used for English OOD fallback and some non-English languages" — [kokoro README](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/README.md)
- No cloning (preset voices) [S] — [findskill.ai](https://findskill.ai/blog/best-open-source-tts-2026/)

**Supertonic 3 (Supertone)** [P]
- Repository is now an archive ("No updates, bug fixes, security patches, or support will be provided"); the archive "does not include hosted demos or Voice Builder services" — [supertonic README](https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic/blob/main/README.md)

- Code MIT — [supertonic LICENSE](https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic/blob/main/LICENSE). Weights licence not verified.

### Inferences
- For a codec running at 50 tokens/s (NeuCodec), real time needs ≥50 tok/s from the LM. NeuTTS-Air at 20 tok/s on a Galaxy A25 is ~0.4x real time and Nano at 45 tok/s is just under 1x before codec cost; on a Ryzen HX 370 or M4 CPU both are 2–4x real time. So NeuTTS is a laptop engine, borderline on mid-range Android.
- Two of the most attractive permissively licensed small engines (Chatterbox, Supertonic) were abandoned by their vendors during 2026. Their MIT code remains usable, but the project would own maintenance. Pocket TTS (three tech reports and new models in Sep–Oct 2026) and KittenTTS are the actively developed ones.
- Pocket TTS is the only shortlisted model that combines cloning, token-level streaming, no phonemizer, permissive code licence and proven CPU-only operation. Its weakness is expressiveness: no emotion tags are documented.
- The combination "own voice + emotion tags + truly small" does not exist off the shelf in permissive form: Chatterbox-Nano has tags + cloning but is English-only and unmaintained; NeuTTS-2E has emotions but fixed speakers; KittenTTS 2 has both but is 1.7B with a custom weights licence.

### Gaps
- No verified MOS / arena numbers for any model; official TTS Arena and Artificial Analysis pages were unreachable. "Breeze TTS 2" and "Voxtral TTS" could not be confirmed from a primary source.
- Weights licences for Pocket TTS, Chatterbox, Supertonic, CosyVoice, Orpheus, Marvis, MOSS-TTS-Nano, Soprano, OmniVoice, and the text of the Stellon Labs Community License were not readable (Hugging Face blocked).
- Kyutai's Sep/Oct 2026 Pocket TTS blog posts (quality of the 2M/7M/18M models, whether the tiny models clone voices) could not be opened: [kyutai.org post](https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-10-20-pocket-tts-small).
- No figures found for OuteTTS, Parler-TTS, MaskGCT, Dia v1, MeloTTS beyond what was already known; not re-researched in depth.

## 2. What runs on tablets/phones vs laptops; web view (WebGPU/WASM) vs native

### Takeaway
Phones and tablets can run the ≤100M class natively in real time (Pocket TTS, Kokoro, Supertonic, small Kitten models); the 0.3–0.6B cloning LMs are laptop-class; ≥1.5B models need Apple-silicon GPU or a discrete GPU. Inside a web view, Kokoro (transformers.js), Pocket TTS (WASM/ONNX Runtime Web), Supertonic (onnxruntime-web/WebGPU) and sherpa-onnx WASM builds exist, but mobile browser numbers are thin and native plugins (Core ML/MLX on Apple, ONNX Runtime/LiteRT on Android, a sidecar binary on desktop) are the safer route.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple native (Core ML / ANE)**: FluidAudio (Apache-2.0 Swift SDK) ships Kokoro on the Neural Engine, PocketTTS streaming with cloning (6 languages, 6-layer and 24-layer variants), Pocket TTS 18m/7m/2m and Phonon 31m (macOS 15 / iOS 18), Supertonic-3 (4-stage Core ML pipeline), StyleTTS2 zero-shot, Chatterbox Multilingual and Nano — [FluidAudio README](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/README.md), [FluidAudio Models.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Models.md)
- FluidAudio showcase claims (X posts, not independently verified): PocketTTS "running fast on the ANE & background-capable" on iOS; "Supertonic-3 running on iPhone 17 Pro via ANE/CoreML with 2 minutes of audio generated in 3 seconds" — [FluidAudio README](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/README.md)
- FluidAudio negative results: KittenTTS "Not supported due to inefficient espeak alternatives"; Qwen3-TTS Core ML port "Now 1.1GB but too slow" — [FluidAudio Models.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Models.md)

### Inferences
- Device-class fit (derived from the figures above):
  - Mid-range Android tablet: Pocket TTS (≈1x real time on Pixel 8a GPU via LiteRT, so the 100M model is marginal; the 18M/7M models or 6-layer variants are the safer choice), Kokoro, Supertonic 3, Kitten small models. NeuTTS-Nano is borderline; nothing ≥0.3B is realistic.
  - Recent iPad/iPhone: Pocket TTS and Kokoro on ANE/MLX comfortably; Chatterbox-Nano (110M) via Core ML plausible; Marvis (310M) claimed.
  - GPU-less Windows laptop: Pocket TTS (expect ~2–6x real time depending on CPU), Chatterbox-Nano (3x on 8 cores), NeuTTS Air/Nano GGUF (2–4x on a recent Ryzen), Kokoro/Supertonic ONNX. KittenTTS 2 needs ~6 GB RAM and its CPU real-time claim has no published per-CPU numbers in the files read.
  - Apple-silicon Mac: all of the above plus KittenTTS 2 (MLX), Qwen3-TTS 0.6B, Chatterbox 0.5B, CSM/Marvis. VoxCPM2 is slower than real time on an M4 Pro (RTF ~1.76).
- NPUs on Copilot+ PCs are not a ready TTS target: no TTS project read here ships a QNN/NPU build; CPU via ONNX Runtime or llama.cpp is the dependable path on Windows.
- For Electron, a sidecar binary (Pocket TTS Rust, llama.cpp + ONNX decoder) avoids WebGPU variability; for Capacitor, a native plugin wrapping FluidAudio (iOS) and sherpa-onnx or LiteRT (Android) is the lowest-risk design. WASM in the web view is a fallback, not the primary engine, on mobile.

### Gaps
- No trustworthy time-to-first-audio measurements on iPad, mid-range Android or a GPU-less Windows laptop for any cloning model; the only first-audio figures are vendor ones on M4 (Pocket TTS ~200 ms) or server GPUs.
- RAM footprints at runtime are mostly unpublished (Soprano "<1 GB", Marvis "1GB RAM" minimum, KittenTTS 2 "about 6 GB").
- No ExecuTorch builds were found in the sources read.

## 3. Personal voice options: Apple, Android, Microsoft; per-user fine-tuning from historical audio

### Takeaway
Apple Personal Voice is the only OS-level personal voice usable by third-party apps (via `AVSpeechSynthesizer` after user authorisation), now created from 10 phrases on Apple-Intelligence-class devices, but it must be recorded live by a person who can still speak, covers few languages, and is not available on Windows/Android. Android and Windows have no equivalent on-device feature; Azure's personal voice is cloud-only and its code of conduct forbids pre-existing recordings. For historical recordings the practical path is zero-shot cloning from a cleaned 10–30 s reference, optionally followed by a one-off fine-tune (hours of data ideal; GPU usually required).

### Cited Findings
**Apple Personal Voice**
- iOS 26: setup uses 10 randomised phrases instead of 150 and generates a voice "in under a minute" (one outlet says under two minutes); a short three-word-phrase option exists for people who cannot read full sentences; created on device [S] — [9to5Mac, 19 Mar 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/19/ios-26-made-one-of-iphones-wildest-most-unique-features-a-lot-better/), [Apple accessibility page](https://www.apple.com/accessibility/)
- On an iPhone 14 Pro Max ("non-AI enabled") the process is reportedly still 150 phrases (user report) [S] — [AppleVis forum](https://applevis.com/forum/ios-ipados/interesting-observation-personal-voice-if-non-ai-enabled-phone-used)
- Languages quoted from Apple's footnote: English (United States), Spanish (Mexico), Chinese (Mandarin, China mainland); a user reports UK English missing [S] — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1044205), [Apple Discussions](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256263339)
- Third-party access: apps call `AVSpeechSynthesizer.requestPersonalVoiceAuthorization`, then select voices whose traits include `.isPersonalVoice`; the user must enable "Allow Apps to Request to Use"; apps "can request to speak with the voice but can't capture audio from it"; AAC apps using it include Spoken, TD Snap, Proloquo, LAMP [S] — [WWDC23 session 10033 notes](https://wwdcnotes.com/notes/wwdc23/10033), [spokenaac.com](https://spokenaac.com/aac-apps-with-personal-voice/), [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/accessibility-platform-features)
- Known problems (developer forum reports) [S]: authorisation may need an app restart if synthesis ran before authorisation (iOS 18.x) — [Apple forum 764814](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/764814); iOS 26 beta bugs where a Personal Voice produced no audio or an English voice switched to Spanish/Chinese — [forum 792409](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/792409), [forum 797225](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/797225); `AVSpeechSynthesisVoice(language:)` ignoring user-selected voices in iOS 26.0/26.1 beta — [forum 804648](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804648); AVSpeechSynthesizer failing with some voices on "Mac (Designed for iPad)" — [forum 841198](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/841198)

**Android / Google**
- No on-device personal voice clone feature or TextToSpeech API for custom cloned voices was found; Project Relate is personalised speech recognition for atypical speech, not a voice clone; third-party TTS engines can register as the system TTS engine (a Piper-based engine is in beta testing) [S] — [9to5Google Project Relate](https://9to5google.com/guides/project-relate), [AppleVis Piper for Android thread](https://www.applevis.com/comment/219841)

**Microsoft**
- Azure personal voice is a Limited Access cloud feature requiring registration and a recorded consent statement from the speaker; the code of conduct requires technical controls so users "record their training data recording scripts in your application, and cannot use any pre-existing recordings to train a personal voice model" [S] — [Azure limited access](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/cognitive-services/speech-service/text-to-speech/limited-access), [Azure TTS code of conduct](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/cognitive-services/speech-service/tts-code-of-conduct), [personal voice overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-services/speech-service/personal-voice-overview)

**Per-user fine-tuning and data preparation (open tooling)**
- Emilia-Pipe (Amphion, MIT repo): open pipeline for in-the-wild audio: (1) source separation to remove background music (UVR-MDX-NET-Inst_HQ_3), (2) speaker diarization (pyannote 3.1), (3) VAD segmentation into 3–30 s single-speaker segments (Silero), (4) ASR (WhisperX medium), with DNSMOS P.835 quality scoring; "can process one hour of raw audio into model-ready data in just a few minutes" — [Emilia-Pipe README](https://github.com/open-mmlab/Amphion/blob/main/preprocessors/Emilia/README.md)
- WhisperX (BSD-2-Clause) for transcription with word-level alignment — [whisperX LICENSE](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX/blob/main/LICENSE); resemble-enhance (MIT) for denoising/enhancement — [resemble-enhance LICENSE](https://github.com/resemble-ai/resemble-enhance/blob/main/LICENSE)
- Kyutai recommends enhancing the reference sample before cloning "because the audio quality of the sample is also reproduced" (it points to Adobe's cloud enhancer) — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Zero-shot reference lengths: NeuTTS 3–15 s; KittenTTS 2 5–30 s; Qwen3-TTS 3 s; Marvis 10 s; Zonos 10–30 s; Fish S2-Pro 10–30 s; OmniVoice 3–10 s ("Longer audio slows down inference and may degrade cloning quality") — sources as cited in section 1; [OmniVoice README](https://github.com/k2-fsa/OmniVoice/blob/master/README.md)
- **Piper/VITS fine-tune**: dataset is `wav|text` CSV; fine-tuning from an existing checkpoint "is recommended since it will speed up training a lot, even if the checkpoint is from a different language"; the training guide's table gives ~2,000 epochs for fine-tunes with under ~5 h of data and 650–850 for ~10–15 h; text is phonemised by espeak-ng by default (a raw phoneme-id mode exists) — [piper1-gpl TRAINING.md](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/blob/main/docs/TRAINING.md). Piper engine is GPL-3.0-or-later and "embeds espeak-ng"; the project makes no licence grant over voice models — [piper1-gpl README](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/blob/main/README.md), [LICENSING.md](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/blob/main/docs/LICENSING.md)
- **NeuTTS fine-tune**: example script; a 10 h dataset needs ~1,000–2,000 steps — [neutts TRAINING.md](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/TRAINING.md)
- **Qwen3-TTS**: Base models support single-speaker fine-tuning from a JSONL of `audio`, `text`, `ref_audio` — [Qwen3-TTS finetuning README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/main/finetuning/README.md)
- **VoxCPM** (all versions): SFT and LoRA fine-tuning supported — [VoxCPM README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM/blob/main/README.md)
- **Orpheus**: fine-tune scripts; LoRA via unsloth notebook; warns that synthetic training data gives worse voice fine-tunes — [Orpheus README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS/blob/main/README.md)
- **Pocket TTS training**: official support is Linux + one NVIDIA GPU (~16 GB with reduced batch size); from scratch needs 200 h for intelligible and 2,000 h for production-level speech; the documented use case is new languages, not per-speaker fine-tuning — [pocket-tts training README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/training/README.md)
- **Supertonic**: per-user voices were only obtainable from the hosted Voice Builder, which is not part of the archive — [supertonic README](https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic/blob/main/README.md)
- Commercial reference point (cloud): ElevenLabs Impact Program gives people with MND/ALS (and other permanent voice loss) free access, creating a professional voice clone "using past recordings, home videos, or other audio archives"; its Impact Voice Lab uses volunteers to "clean and prepare old audio recordings" [S] — [ElevenLabs impact announcement](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/impact-program-announcement), [Impact Voice Lab](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/impact-voice-lab)
- UK reference point: SpeakUnique lists a minimum of 150 sentences or 2 minutes of existing audio (May 2025 chart; a Feb 2025 chart said 7 minutes); prices £175 voice build, £250 voice repair, £300 voice design, free for people with MND via MND Association funding [S] — [MND Association chart May 2025](https://www.mndassociation.org/sites/default/files/public/2025-05/Voice%20banking%20services%20-%20most%20popular%20comparison%20charts%20-%20May%202025.pdf), [MND Association chart Oct 2025](https://www.mndassociation.org/sites/default/files/public/2025-10/Voice%20banking%20services%20comparison%20chart%20-%20Oct%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Azure's rule against pre-existing recordings shows that mainstream consent designs assume the speaker can record live; an AAC app serving people who have already lost speech needs a different consent mechanism (see section 6).
- Data requirement tiers that follow from the sources: (a) 3–30 s clean reference → zero-shot clone, runs anywhere the model runs, quality bounded by the reference; (b) 5–60 min → useful for choosing the best reference clips, building several style references (calm, animated, quiet), and small LoRA/SFT on 0.5B-class models; (c) several hours → Piper-style or LM fine-tunes as the vendors document them. No source read gives measured quality for 5–60 minutes of fine-tuning data.
- Fine-tuning tooling in every repo read assumes PyTorch + NVIDIA GPU; none documents fine-tuning on a GPU-less Windows laptop. An Apple-silicon Mac (MPS/MLX) is the only consumer device where a local one-off job is plausible, and that is unproven in these sources. Zero-shot cloning plus exported voice state is the only path that certainly works fully on-device for all four device classes.
- Kokoro "voice packs" for a new speaker: nothing in the primary sources read documents training a Kokoro voice from user audio; treat as unsupported.

### Gaps
- Apple's official language list, device requirements and macOS behaviour for Personal Voice in iOS/macOS 26–27 could not be read from Apple (support/developer sites blocked). No information found on iOS 27 changes.
- No quantitative study found on clone quality vs amount/quality of historical audio (voicemail-band 8 kHz audio in particular).
- Fully offline, permissively licensed speech enhancement suited to voicemail-quality audio was not evaluated; resemble-enhance and UVR licences for model weights not verified.

## 4. Cloning from degraded or dysarthric speech ("voice repair")

### Takeaway
Voice repair is an established idea (Edinburgh/CSTR, now SpeakUnique) and a paid service, but open, automatic reconstruction of a pre-illness voice from dysarthric recordings is not solved: the one recent study with a large open model (Parler-TTS, Interspeech 2025) reports inconsistent intelligibility control and speaker identity. Zero-shot cloners reproduce what they hear, including dysarthria and noise, so pre-illness recordings remain the best source.

### Cited Findings
- Edinburgh voice repair: "Repair is achieved by substituting or interpolating parts of the user model with corresponding parts of the average voice model. Initially, this required expert speech therapists to decide which parts to repair" (HMM-era method) [S] — [CSTR publication](https://www.cstr.ed.ac.uk/downloads/publications/2012/AST-33_1.pdf)
- SpeakUnique (Edinburgh spin-out): "voice repair" is for people who have "already noticed a deterioration in their speech, such as slowness, slurring or a change in vocal quality"; NHS comparison describes it as "voice synthesis with editing to remove some dysarthric features"; "voice design" can use old recordings that meet quality criteria, or blend donor voices [S] — [EKHUFT NHS comparison](https://www.ekhuft.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/services/radiological-sciences/medical-physics/kmcat/public/voicebanking/compare-options/), [assistive.co.nz SpeakUnique](https://assistive.co.nz/product/speakunique/), [University of Edinburgh spin-out news](https://www.ed.ac.uk/node/112700)
- Sanchez & King, Interspeech 2025, "Can we reconstruct a dysarthric voice with the large speech model Parler TTS?": fine-tuned Parler-TTS Mini v0.1 for 2 epochs to approximate a dysarthric speaker's pre-onset voice; the model "struggles to control intelligibility and to maintain consistent speaker identity"; some expert listeners heard outputs similar to the target speaker with higher intelligibility than their natural recordings, but full reconstruction to a healthy voice was not consistently achieved; the text-prompt mechanism gives insufficient control [S] — [arXiv 2506.04397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04397v1), [ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/sanchez25_interspeech.html), [Edinburgh research portal](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/can-we-reconstruct-a-dysarthric-voice-with-the-large-speech-model/)
- Moëll & Aronsson (Mar 2025): commercial voice cloning applied to TORGO dysarthric speakers preserved the disordered characteristics, to the point that a speech-language pathologist found synthetic clips close to real dysarthric speech — i.e. cloning copies dysarthria [S] — [arXiv 2503.01266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01266)
- Clinical guidance: start banking within weeks of diagnosis; synthesis trained on dysarthric speech tends to produce dysarthric output [S] — [voxbooster voice banking guide](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-banking-medical-patients); University of Iowa guide lists a commercial "Voice Repair" option ($335) and voice design from old recordings [S] — [Iowa Voice Banking Guide](https://csd.uiowa.edu/sites/csd.uiowa.edu/files/2025-08/Voice_Banking_Guide.pdf)
- First-person account of a bulbar-onset ALS patient relying on a clone made before speech loss: "It genuinely sounded like me" [S] — [Maclean's](https://macleans.ca/technology-3/how-ai-gave-me-my-voice-back/)
- MIT Technology Review (Feb 2026) reports on a musician with ALS using AI to sing again (ElevenLabs-related coverage; article not opened) [S] — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/02/13/1132913/als-stole-this-musicians-voice-ai-sing/amp/)
- ElevenLabs Impact Program builds clones from "past recordings, home videos, or other audio archives" with volunteer audio clean-up; no dysarthria-specific repair described [S] — [ElevenLabs impact](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/impact-program-announcement), [Impact Voice Lab](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/impact-voice-lab)
- Google Project Relate / Euphonia: personalised recognition of atypical speech; no released voice-repair TTS found [S] — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/guides/project-relate)

### Inferences
- Practical order of preference for an open app: (1) pre-illness recordings, cleaned, as zero-shot references; (2) the least-affected current recordings, selecting short fluent segments (timbre is carried by a few seconds; rate and articulation come from the model, so short clean vowels-rich segments may transfer identity without the dysarthric timing — untested here); (3) voice design/blending with a relative's voice as fallback.
- Models that separate speaker embedding from prosody prompt are structurally better suited to "repair": Qwen3-TTS `x_vector_only_mode` uses only the speaker embedding ([Qwen3-TTS README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/main/README.md)); KittenTTS 2 feeds identity by two routes (speaker embedding and codec-token prompt) ([KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md)); IndexTTS-2 disentangles timbre from emotion ([index-tts README](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts/blob/main/README.md)). Continuation-style cloners (prompt audio + transcript) will copy dysarthric rhythm.
- A custom pipeline could use a large embedding-conditioned model offline to synthesise clean speech in the person's timbre from dysarthric input, then use that clean synthetic audio as the reference for the small on-device model. No source read validates this; Orpheus warns synthetic data degrades fine-tunes.

### Gaps
- Full text of the Sanchez & King paper and the CSTR paper could not be opened; numbers are missing.
- No open-source tool specifically for dysarthric voice repair was found. No evidence found on how Apple Personal Voice behaves with dysarthric input, beyond the short-phrase recording option.
- The Voice Keeper, Acapela my-own-voice, ModelTalker and Team Gleason details were not retrieved.

## 5. Expressiveness for AAC

### Takeaway
AAC users have long reported that they cannot convey emotion, irony or emphasis with synthetic voices; 2025–2026 open models finally expose usable controls (inline laughter/sigh tags, emotion tags or vectors, natural-language style instructions, emphasis spans), but in small on-device models these controls are limited, beta-quality, or tied to fixed speakers. Context-conditioned prosody exists in the CSM/Marvis/Dia2 family, which accept prior turns' text and audio.

### Cited Findings
**User needs**
- Microsoft Research "Expressive Keyboard" work: AAC users are dissatisfied with the expressivity and tone of the synthesised voice; proposed emoji and punctuation as controls (older work) [S] — [Microsoft Research PDF](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2018/01/voicesetting.pdf)
- AAC users "cannot manipulate the synthesized speech to convey an emotional state, irony, or emphasis" (study on fundamental frequency and speech rate) [S] — [PMC6802860](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6802860)
- Noufi, May & Berger (Frontiers in Computer Science, Sep 2025) propose a "vocal persona" framework for paralinguistic control, arguing it could "enhance user agency and embodiment" for AAC users [S] — [Frontiers article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1575296/full)
- "It's Complicated: On the Design and Evaluation of AI-Powered AAC Interfaces" recommends evaluating generated prosody with both AAC users and their communication partners [S] — [arXiv 2606.24854](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24854)
- A survey of 53 AAC users on cultural/linguistic identity found American and British accents dominate available voices [S] — [arXiv 2605.24337](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24337v1)

**Controls available in open models**
- Inline non-verbal tags: Chatterbox Turbo/Nano `[cough] [laugh] [chuckle]` — [chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/README.md); Supertonic 3 `<laugh> <breath> <sigh>` (10 tags) — [supertonic README](https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic/blob/main/README.md); KittenTTS 2 ten vocal events — [voices-and-expression.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/voices-and-expression.md); OmniVoice `[laughter]` — [OmniVoice README](https://github.com/k2-fsa/OmniVoice/blob/master/README.md); Orpheus emotion tags — [Orpheus README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS/blob/main/README.md)
- Emotion selection: NeuTTS-2E (6 emotions + neutral, fixed speakers) — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md); KittenTTS 2 leading `[emotion]` tag (ten recognised, beta) and `(((emphasis)))` — [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md); IndexTTS-2 8-float emotion vector, emotion reference audio and `emo_alpha` intensity — [index-tts README](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts/blob/main/README.md); Zonos sliders for rate, pitch variation, happiness/anger/sadness/fear — [Zonos README](https://github.com/Zyphra/Zonos/blob/main/README.md)
- Intensity knobs: original Chatterbox `exaggeration` and `cfg_weight` (higher exaggeration speeds speech; lower cfg slows it) — [chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/README.md)
- Natural-language style: Qwen3-TTS `instruct` (e.g. "Very happy."), adaptive tone from text semantics — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/main/README.md); VoxCPM2 "(slightly faster, cheerful tone)" prefix with cloned timbre — [VoxCPM README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM/blob/main/README.md); Fish S2-Pro/Drama 3 free-form tags `[whisper] [angry] [laughing] [excited] [pause]` — [fish-speech README](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/blob/main/README.md); CosyVoice instructions for emotion, speed, volume — [CosyVoice README](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/blob/main/README.md)
- Whisper: Zonos notes audio prefixes "can be used to elicit behaviours such as whispering which can otherwise be challenging to replicate when cloning from speaker embeddings" — [Zonos README](https://github.com/Zyphra/Zonos/blob/main/README.md); Step-Audio-EditX edits an existing utterance to whisper/emotion iteratively — [Step-Audio-EditX README](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-EditX/blob/main/README.md)
- Style follows the reference: OuteTTS "inherits the referenced speaker's emotion, style, and accent" — [OuteTTS README](https://github.com/edwko/OuteTTS/blob/main/README.md)
- Speed: Kokoro `speed` parameter — [kokoro README](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/README.md); Kitten small models `speed` — [kitten-tts-2-tiny.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/kitten-tts-2-tiny.md)

**Conversation-context prosody**
- Sesame CSM "leverages the history of the conversation to produce more natural and coherent speech"; works best when prior utterances (text + audio) are passed as context; only CSM-1B is open, the larger demo model is not [S] — [Sesame research post](https://sesame.com/research), [learnprompting explainer](https://learnprompting.org/blog/sesame-conversational-speech-model); open base model is not fine-tuned on a specific voice — [csm README](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm/blob/main/README.md)
- Marvis (CSM architecture, 250M+60M) processes "interleaved text and audio sequences" and targets on-device use — [marvis-tts README](https://github.com/Marvis-Labs/marvis-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Dia2: "You can condition the output on audio, enabling natural conversations in realtime" — [dia2 README](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia2/blob/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- Because cloners inherit style from the reference, a practical on-device expressiveness mechanism that needs no special model is **multiple voice states per person** (neutral, warm/animated, quiet, firm) built from differently-styled segments of their historical audio and exported once (Pocket TTS voice states make this cheap). The user picks a "tone" key; this maps well onto eye-gaze UI. Not validated in any source.
- Volume and shouting are best handled as post-processing gain plus a style reference; no small model read exposes a loudness control.
- Context-aware prosody on-device is feasible only in the Marvis/CSM-size class (≥300M) and would require feeding the partner's speech (ASR text or audio) as context; this is research-grade for AAC, with no user study found.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 user study was found that tests emotion-tag or style-prompt TTS with AAC users in live conversation.
- No measurement of how reliably small-model emotion tags work with a cloned (rather than built-in) voice.

## 6. Safety and consent

### Takeaway
Open models rely on licence/README clauses requiring consent and, in a few cases, default-on watermarks (Perth in Chatterbox and NeuTTS); none verifies consent technically. Commercial services use attestation plus live read-aloud verification, which does not work for people who can no longer speak; programmes for ALS accept historical recordings through a managed human process.

### Cited Findings
- Watermarking: Chatterbox embeds Resemble's Perth watermark in every output, claimed to survive MP3 compression and editing with "nearly 100% detection accuracy"; Perth is MIT — [chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/README.md), [Perth LICENSE](https://github.com/resemble-ai/Perth/blob/master/LICENSE). NeuTTS watermarks by default with Perth, but watermarking is silently disabled if the dependency is missing — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md). Orpheus recommends Silent Cipher watermarking as an optional step — [Orpheus README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS/blob/main/README.md)
- Consent clauses in open projects: Pocket TTS prohibits "voice impersonation or cloning without explicit and lawful consent" — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md); CSM: do not mimic "real individuals without their explicit consent" — [csm README](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm/blob/main/README.md); Dia2: no audio "resembling real individuals without permission" — [dia2 README](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia2/blob/main/README.md); Spark-TTS forbids unauthorised cloning and names "assistive technologies" as a legitimate use — [Spark-TTS README](https://github.com/SparkAudio/Spark-TTS/blob/main/README.md); Marvis: "Obtain appropriate consent and permissions before deployment" — [marvis-tts README](https://github.com/Marvis-Labs/marvis-tts/blob/main/README.md); VibeVoice warns of deepfake misuse and recommends against commercial/real-world use without further testing — [VibeVoice README](https://github.com/microsoft/VibeVoice/blob/main/README.md)
- Microsoft Azure personal voice: explicit consent with a recorded statement from the speaker; Limited Access registration; pre-existing recordings must be technically blocked [S] — [Azure TTS code of conduct](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/cognitive-services/speech-service/tts-code-of-conduct), [limited access](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/cognitive-services/speech-service/text-to-speech/limited-access)
- ElevenLabs: consent attestation for every clone; Professional Voice Cloning adds a verification step where the speaker reads a displayed/random prompt that is matched against the samples ("voice captcha") [S] — [ElevenLabs voice cloning API blog](https://elevenlabs.io/ko/blog/voice-cloning-api), [ConductAtlas policy record](https://conductatlas.com/platform/elevenlabs/elevenlabs-safety-policy/provision/CA-P-062107/professional-voice-cloning-verification-required/)
- Consumer Reports (Mar 2025) criticised several voice-cloning products for weak safeguards (article not opened; details unverified) [S] — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/03/10/ai_voice_cloning_safeguards/)
- Apple Personal Voice: created and kept on device unless the user shares it across their iCloud devices; apps need explicit authorisation and cannot capture the audio [S] — [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/accessibility-platform-features), [Apple accessibility](https://www.apple.com/accessibility/)
- Biometric law: applicability of voiceprint statutes (e.g. BIPA) "varies by jurisdiction and depends on how 'voiceprint' is defined"; whether a given consent flow meets GDPR explicit-consent or BIPA written-consent standards is flagged as open [S] — [ConductAtlas ElevenLabs record](https://conductatlas.com/platform/elevenlabs/elevenlabs-usage-policy/voice-cloning-without-consent-prohibition/)

### Inferences
- For this project the defensible design is: voice models and reference audio never leave the device; stored encrypted with OS keystore; a recorded or documented consent artefact (from the person, or a legally authorised representative when the person cannot speak or record) stored alongside the voice; clear provenance metadata; optional default-on watermark (Perth is MIT and reusable outside Chatterbox).
- Watermarking a person's own everyday speech is debatable for AAC (it marks a disabled person's speech as "synthetic"); it protects mostly against exfiltrated voice models. This trade-off is not discussed in any source read.
- Shipping a general-purpose cloner inside an app-store app raises review risk; restricting enrolment to a guided, consent-recorded flow reduces it. No app-store policy text was retrieved.

### Gaps
- No primary legal source on treating locally stored voice models as biometric/special-category data was retrieved; needs legal review.
- No published consent protocol found for cloning from historical recordings on behalf of someone who can no longer speak (ElevenLabs Impact process details not available).
- App Store / Play policy on voice cloning not researched.

## 7. Custom engineering beyond off-the-shelf

### Takeaway
The building blocks for a stronger custom system exist in permissive form: text-stream-in/audio-stream-out engines, cached voice states, deterministic seeds for phrase caching, permissive G2P (misaki, gruut, small neural G2P), and published distillation recipes down to ~2M parameters. The main unproven piece is per-user distillation of an expressive large model into a small streaming one.

### Cited Findings
**Streaming while the user is still composing**
- Qwen3-TTS "can output the first audio packet immediately after a single character is input" (dual-track streaming) — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/main/README.md)
- Dia2 "does not need the entire text to produce the audio, and can start generating as the first few words are given" — [dia2 README](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia2/blob/main/README.md)
- VibeVoice-Realtime supports streaming text input with ~300 ms first audible latency — [VibeVoice README](https://github.com/microsoft/VibeVoice/blob/main/README.md); CosyVoice supports text-in and audio-out streaming — [CosyVoice README](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/blob/main/README.md); Orpheus latency is "reducible to ~100ms with input streaming" — [Orpheus README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS/blob/main/README.md)
- kokoro-js `TextSplitterStream` lets text be pushed incrementally while audio chunks stream out — [kokoro.js README](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/kokoro.js/README.md)
- Counter-example: KittenTTS 2 only yields a chunk after generating and vocoding it in full — [KittenTTS README](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/README.md)

**Caching / precomputation**
- Pocket TTS voice state export (`export-voice` → safetensors kv-cache) makes voice loading "very fast"; model and voice states should be kept in memory — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Reusable clone prompts: Qwen3-TTS `create_voice_clone_prompt` — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/main/README.md); OmniVoice `VoiceClonePrompt.load` — [OmniVoice README](https://github.com/k2-fsa/OmniVoice/blob/master/README.md); NeuTTS pre-encoded reference codes — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)
- NeuTTS generation is sampled and seedable: "the same inputs and seed always produce identical audio, on both the PyTorch and GGUF backbones, batch or streaming", so a liked take can be regenerated or cached — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md)

**Distillation / shrinking**
- Kyutai published small English Pocket TTS models at 18.2M, 6.9M and 1.9M parameters (Oct 2026, "The tiny TTS was actually 10x too big") plus reward-fine-tuned variants, and open training code following the CALM paper — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/README.md), [training README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/blob/main/training/README.md)
- Chatterbox Turbo distilled its speech-token-to-mel decoder from 10 steps to one — [chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/README.md)
- KittenTTS 2 stores ternary weights at 1.6 bits each (1.7B params in 492–954 MB) — [mlx.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/mlx.md)
- Marvis reports total pretraining + fine-tuning cost of $246.69 on one GH200 for a 310M CSM-style model — [marvis-tts README](https://github.com/Marvis-Labs/marvis-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- Caution: Orpheus authors recommend "not using synthetic data for training as it produces worse results when you try to finetune specific voices" — [Orpheus README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS/blob/main/README.md)

**G2P without espeak-ng**
- misaki (Apache-2.0): Kokoro's G2P; English via lexicon + spaCy with espeak only as optional fallback; also Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Vietnamese; TODO lists training seq2seq fallback models and homograph disambiguation — [misaki README](https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki/blob/main/README.md), [misaki LICENSE](https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki/blob/main/LICENSE)
- KittenTTS English front end = misaki lexicon → CMUdict → 0.75M-parameter BART G2P for unknown words (other languages still espeak-ng) — [kitten-tts-2-tiny.md](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS/blob/main/docs/kitten-tts-2-tiny.md)
- gruut (MIT): lexicon + pre-trained G2P model phonemizer with SSML support for several languages — [gruut README](https://github.com/rhasspy/gruut/blob/master/README.md), [gruut LICENSE](https://github.com/rhasspy/gruut/blob/master/LICENSE)
- OpenPhonemizer (BSD-3-Clause Clear; English only; 93.5 on its espeak-similarity benchmark vs 85 for DeepPhonemizer) is "no longer being maintained", its autoregressive model has a known training bug, and it notes dependencies under other licences "notably LGPL" — [OpenPhonemizer README](https://github.com/NeuralVox/OpenPhonemizer/blob/main/README.md)
- FluidAudio ships Core ML G2P assets: a G2P encoder/decoder used by Kokoro/StyleTTS2, a ~1 MB "LuxTTS G2P resource bundle", and a multilingual Charsiu ByT5 G2P Core ML model — [FluidAudio Models.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Models.md), [FluidAudio README](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/README.md)
- Models with no phonemizer at all: Pocket TTS (text tokens) — [FluidAudio Models.md](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Models.md); NeuTTS-2E (text input) — [neutts README](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/README.md); LLM-style models such as Qwen3-TTS, Chatterbox, KittenTTS 2 (text/BPE tokens) — sources in section 1
- Piper remains GPL-3 with embedded espeak-ng — [piper1-gpl README](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/blob/main/README.md)

**Hybrid recorded + synthetic**
- Message banking preserves "natural prosody and emotion for key phrases" but is limited to pre-recorded phrases (low-authority source) [S] — [sneos AI library page](https://share.sneos.com/ai-library/emerging-tech-banking/voice-banking-ai/chatgpt-vs-deepseek-vs-gemini-voice-banking-ai-1330)

### Inferences
- A feasible two-tier architecture: (1) live engine = small streaming cloner (Pocket TTS class) with exported voice states; (2) optional offline "studio" job on a capable machine using a large expressive model (permissive: Qwen3-TTS 1.7B, VoxCPM2, Chatterbox 0.5B) to pre-render the user's frequent phrases, greetings, names and emotional exclamations in several styles; the app plays cached audio when the typed text matches and falls back to live synthesis otherwise. Real banked recordings slot into the same cache with highest priority.
- Speculative synthesis while composing: synthesise the committed prefix sentence-by-sentence in the background and discard on edit; with a ~200 ms first-chunk engine this makes "Speak" effectively instantaneous for multi-sentence messages. Engines with incremental text input (Qwen3-TTS, Dia2, VibeVoice-Realtime) are too large for tablets, so on small devices this must be done at clause granularity on top of a non-incremental engine.
- Per-user distillation (large expressive teacher → small student for one voice) is plausible given Kyutai's open training code and 2M–18M results, but those small models were trained on hundreds to thousands of hours; a single-speaker student trained on teacher-generated audio has no supporting evidence here and conflicts with the Orpheus warning about synthetic data. Treat as research.
- Level-matching and timbre-matching (same codec/vocoder pass, loudness normalisation) between banked recordings and synthetic speech would be needed to avoid audible switches; no source addressed this.
- Avoiding espeak-ng entirely is simplest by choosing a text-token model; for phoneme models (Kokoro, NeuTTS Air/Nano, small Kitten) English can be covered permissively with misaki + a small neural fallback, but other languages still lack a maintained permissive G2P.

### Gaps
- No published result on per-speaker distillation into a sub-50M streaming model, or on quality of the Pocket TTS 2M/7M/18M models with cloned voices.
- Clinical literature on combining message banking with synthesis (e.g. Boston Children's protocol) was not retrieved.
- "NextPhonemizer" (announced successor to OpenPhonemizer) repository was not found (404).
